# Module 3: Humanoid Robotics Overview

## Module Overview

Humanoid robots represent one of the most ambitious applications of Physical AI. By designing robots with human-like bodies, we can deploy them in environments built for humans—using tools, navigating stairs, and collaborating with people.

This module surveys the landscape of humanoid robotics, from cutting-edge platforms like Boston Dynamics' Atlas and Tesla's Optimus to the fundamental challenges of bipedal locomotion and morphological design.

---

## Learning Outcomes

By completing this module, you will be able to:

### 1. Identify Major Humanoid Robot Platforms and Their Capabilities

**What you'll master**:
- Survey leading humanoid robots (Atlas, Optimus, Figure 01, and more)
- Compare capabilities: locomotion, manipulation, sensing
- Understand design tradeoffs across platforms
- Recognize which platforms excel in which domains

**Why it matters**: Knowing the current state of humanoid technology helps you understand what's possible today and what remains challenging.

---

### 2. Understand the Challenges Unique to Bipedal Locomotion

**What you'll master**:
- Explain why bipedal walking is fundamentally difficult
- Describe balance, stability, and dynamic control
- Understand the role of zero-moment point (ZMP) and similar concepts
- Recognize different locomotion strategies (static vs. dynamic)

**Why it matters**: Bipedal locomotion is a defining challenge for humanoid robots—solving it unlocks deployment in human environments.

---

### 3. Describe Human-Robot Morphological Similarities and Differences

**What you'll master**:
- Compare human and robot skeletal/kinematic structures
- Understand degrees of freedom and actuation choices
- Explain material and weight distribution differences
- Recognize when human-like design is beneficial vs. limiting

**Why it matters**: Morphology shapes capabilities—understanding design choices helps you evaluate and improve humanoid systems.

---

## Module Structure

### 1. Major Platforms

### 2. Bipedal Locomotion
